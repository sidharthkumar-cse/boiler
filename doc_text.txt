1. Executive Summary
This report documents the Touchpoint 3 (Prototype Build, Progress & Testing) submission for the Thermax Innomax Innovation Challenge 2026. BoilerCore is a fully operational physics-based digital twin of a 6.1-litre industrial steam boiler, integrating first-principles thermodynamic modelling, real-time sensor fusion, and a web-based supervisory dashboard.
The prototype solves the actual governing equations of mass, energy, and momentum conservation using a 4-equation Index-1 Differential-Algebraic Equation (DAE) system. At its core, BoilerCore employs a Physics-Informed Hybrid AI architecture: a rigorous first-principles physics model serves as the thermodynamic foundation, and a lightweight machine learning residual model (trained via scikit-learn) is layered on top to correct for minor non-linear, unmodelled dynamics. The hybrid forecast is computed as: hybrid_output = physics_forecast + ML_residual_correction. This approach guarantees physical consistency and thermodynamic validity, while the ML layer continuously improves accuracy by learning from the small systematic errors the pure physics model cannot capture.

2. Problem Statement & Innovation
2.1 The Problem
Industrial steam boilers are critical assets across manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, textiles, and food processing. However, traditional boiler management is entirely reactive: operators respond to alarms after a fault has already occurred. This results in unplanned downtime, safety incidents from overpressure or dry firing, and significant energy waste from sub-optimal combustion and heat transfer.
Key Industry Pain Points:
Boiler failures cause 15–30% of unplanned industrial downtime
Scaling/fouling reduces thermal efficiency by 5–10% per mm of deposit, undetected until maintenance
No real-time prediction: operators cannot anticipate pressure excursions 5–10 minutes ahead
Existing SCADA systems log data but do not model future states
Skilled boiler operators are scarce; new operators risk expensive mistakes during training
2.2 Current Gaps in Existing Solutions
Rule-based SCADA systems: React to sensor thresholds — no prediction capability
Machine-learning models: Pure ML models require large labelled datasets, fail outside training distribution, and are opaque black boxes with no physical guarantees
Commercial digital twins: Cost ₹50L–2Cr per deployment; inaccessible to mid-tier manufacturers
Open-source gap: No validated, parameterisable, hardware-ready physics-based boiler twin exists
2.3 Our Proposed Solution — BoilerTwin
BoilerTwin is a Physics-Informed Hybrid AI digital twin that continuously solves the thermodynamic equations governing the boiler’s behaviour in real-time. Its architecture combines a rigorous first-principles physics model — solving the full 4-equation DAE system — with a lightweight scikit-learn ML residual model that corrects for minor non-linear, unmodelled dynamics. The hybrid forecast is assembled as: hybrid_output = physics_forecast + ML_residual_correction. The system predicts temperature, pressure, and water level 5–25 minutes ahead with >97% accuracy, with thermodynamic validity guaranteed at every step by the underlying conservation laws.

4.6 Solver Implementation
The physics engine solves the C·X = D matrix system at every time step and propagates the state forward using a stiff-stable ODE integrator:
Parameter

Rationale
ODE Solver
scipy.integrate.solve_ivp with Radau
Radau is a 5th-order implicit Runge-Kutta algorithm designed specifically for stiff differential-algebraic equations. We initially attempted standard explicit RK45, but it diverged due to the extreme stiffness of the boiling phase (the abrupt ~1,600× density jump from water to steam). Radau’s implicit formulation handles this stiffness unconditionally; RK45 diverges at density ratios > 100.

12. Zuber–Findlay Drift-Flux Void Fraction (Analytical Derivatives). Void fraction α is computed via the Zuber–Findlay (C₀ = 1.13) drift-flux model, with analytical partial derivatives ∂α/∂φ and ∂α/∂P derived via the chain rule. 13. Compressible Choked Steam Flow (Isentropic Nozzle). Steam outlet mass flow is computed using isentropic nozzle equations, with Mach = 1 choked-flow limiting applied when the pressure ratio across the valve exceeds the critical value. This is essential for accurate steam dome pressure modeling during rapid valve operation.

D. Numerical Methods & Solver Rigor
17. Row Scaling of the C Matrix. Each row of the C matrix is divided by its largest element before solving, reducing the condition number from O(10¹⁴) to O(10⁶). This prevents numerical explosion caused by the ∼1,600× density ratio between liquid water and steam spanning fourteen orders of magnitude. 
Row Scaling — C Matrix Numerical Conditioning
Where: C_scaled = row-scaled coefficient matrix; max(|C_row|) = largest absolute value in each row; condition number kappa(C): from O(10^14) unscaled to O(10^6) scaled; root cause: density ratio rho_w/rho_s approx 1600 at 1 bar creates 14-order-of-magnitude span in matrix entries
18. Radau Implicit Runge–Kutta Solver (Stiff DAE). The Radau method (5th-order implicit Runge–Kutta) from scipy.integrate.solve_ivp natively handles the Index-1 DAE structure of the boiler model. An explicit RK45 solver was tested and diverged due to stiffness; Radau’s implicit formulation is unconditionally stable for this class of system.
Radau Implicit Runge–Kutta — Stiff DAE Solver
Where: Radau: 5th-order implicit Runge-Kutta method; A-stable = stable for all step sizes with stiff problems; L-stable = no numerical oscillation at large time steps; Index-1 DAE = differential equations with one algebraic constraint; eigenvalue spread > 10^6 means explicit methods (RK45) would need millions of micro-steps; implemented via scipy.integrate.solve_ivp with method='Radau'
19. Safety Relief Valve (SRV) Pressure Cap. A hard pressure ceiling of 4 bar is enforced at the solver level, mirroring the mechanical SRV in the physical prototype. This prevents unbounded pressure growth in edge-case scenarios and maintains safe model behavior under all operating conditions. 
Safety Relief Valve (SRV) — Pressure Cap
Where: P = drum absolute pressure [Pa]; P_set = SRV set pressure = 4 bar = 4x10^5 Pa; dP/dt = pressure rate of change [Pa/s]; when triggered: dP/dt clamped to 0, excess steam vented; mirrors the mechanical brass safety valve in the physical prototype
20. Parasitic Leak Model. A pressure-proportional leak term ṁleak = Kleak × Pgauge (bar) is subtracted from the steam dome mass balance, accounting for minor steam losses through valve seats and pipe joints that are present in all real boilers but absent from idealised models. 
Parasitic Leak Model
Where: m_dot_leak = parasitic steam leak rate [kg/s]; K_leak = leak coefficient [kg/(s*bar)]; P_gauge = gauge pressure [bar]; K_leak = 0 for current well-sealed prototype; framework ready for field-calibrated leak coefficients from pressure decay tests

5. Extended Kalman Filter — Sensor-Model Fusion
5.1 Why an EKF?
Raw sensor readings contain noise (PT100: ±0.3°C, pressure transducer: ±0.01 bar). Directly re-initialising the ODE solver with noisy measurements causes prediction 'jumps' that propagate forward and destabilise the forecast. The EKF provides a principled Bayesian optimal estimate by blending sensor observations with physics model predictions, weighted by their respective uncertainties.

8. Iteration, Learning & Engineering Trade-offs
8.1 What Worked Well
Radau solver stability: We initially attempted standard explicit RK45, but it diverged due to the extreme stiffness of the boiling phase — the abrupt ~1,600× density jump from water to steam. Upgrading to the Radau method (a 5th-order implicit Runge-Kutta algorithm designed for stiff DAEs) eliminated all ODE divergence in stiff boiling regimes.
EKF convergence: Filter converged within 5 cycles and maintained stability for the entire 21,364-cycle live session
IAPWS-97 spline tables: Pre-computing thermodynamic properties at 500 points reduced evaluation time by 100×
Row scaling in C matrix: Normalising each equation by its largest element reduced condition number from O(10¹⁴) to O(10⁶)
Live model verification: Automatically comparing forecast vs actual at horizon expiry built operator confidence
Modular Python architecture: Clean separation of physics/, engine/, core/ layers enabled rapid debugging and testing
